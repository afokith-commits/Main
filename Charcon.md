### PILOT PITCH: "MARIANNE" (Subject 088 // Marianne Lindemann)

In U.C. 0093, seventeen-year-old Adrian Sagara, called "Rian" by his family, was a harbor hand on the Sunda Strait. He hauled cargo and tied off boats for his father, who couldn't work the way he used to. Then the Axis impact broke the Sunda Shelf and killed him. His consciousness didn't go anywhere. It drifted in the psychic fallout for two years, until a skirmish in the flooded Sunda Sink left Lt. Soren Hayes dying inside an experimental Psycho-Frame prototype. The machine was built by Anaheim Electronics on a black-budget order from the Vist Foundation, and as Hayes bled out, its Psycho-Field ran away from him. His body dissolved into the bio-amniotic gel in the cockpit. The overcharged Psycommu pulled Adrian's drifting mind into the empty space, and the Augusta-derived medical system rebuilt a body from the stem-cell gel around it: an unspecialized adolescent girl.

Senior Director Dietrich Lindemann of Anaheim came to clean up the wreck and recover the black box. When the hatch opened and the spent gel poured out, there was a girl in the seat, unconscious, lost inside a ruined flight suit. He knew what Anaheim would do with an undocumented Cyber-Newtype, so he had the frame burned down, logged Hayes as dead, and took her home to the Moon. His own daughter Clara had died at school outside Singapore in the Axis impact, and he never got to bury her. In Von Braun City he sheltered the girl behind his estate and his rank, under a new name: "Marianne Lindemann." The household shortened it to "Ria," a common German pet name, and Dietrich said it every morning over coffee without knowing it was the name of the boy she used to be.

By U.C. 0099, Marianne is nineteen and living a polite, careful life in Von Braun. She plays the quiet, delicate heiress because she has to. Dietrich's position is the only thing between her and Anaheim's Internal Disposal Bureau, the "Cleaners," who still hold a standing order on Soren Hayes: liquidate on sight, retrieve the black box. She doesn't think of Hayes as a person. He's more like something left inside her: reflexes she never learned, a body that sometimes moves before she decides to, neural spikes that flare around loaders and reactors.

Then a transmission hits Hayes's old backdoor frequency. It's from the Vist Foundation: **'Power and means to change the future, in exchange for your service.'** Vist has enough leverage over Anaheim's board to wipe the Hayes order and give her a clean civilian identity for good. But if Vist found Hayes's signal in Von Braun, the Cleaners are already close behind.

Marianne knows that staying will bring them to Dietrich's door, so she leaves in the middle of the night without a word. That way he can say, honestly, that he doesn't know where she went. She boards the unmarked freighter *Althea* at Berth 17, Dock 04, bound for Colony SF at Side 4. She means to climb into whatever machine Vist has built, get through their war, and be Soren Hayes one more time. Not to get a past back, but to buy the right to stay Marianne.

#### THE NAME: MARIANNE / RIA / RIAN

The most important thing about this character is a coincidence of letters. Play it slowly and never explain it to her.

**MARIANNE** is classical, soft, aristocratic. It sounds like a girl from an old European family, which is exactly the cover. The name has no weight to it, and she wears it like a coat she doesn't need. But it still has him inside it, and she never notices:

```
M · A · [ R · I · A · N ] · N · E
```

On the first anniversary, Evelyn gives her a silver pendant engraved with **M·A·R·I·A·N·N·E** in full. The four letters in the middle weren't given to her by the family that raised her, and nobody in that house will ever read them. She turns the pendant over with her thumb every night before she sleeps.

**RIA** *(REE-ah)* is the one that hurts. In Germany and France, Marianne is routinely shortened to Ria. It's the standard pet name for a girl in a good family, which is exactly why Dietrich uses it. He says it in the kitchen doorway most mornings with his coat still on his arm: *"Ria, come down, the car is waiting."* It's affectionate, European, ordinary. A father's name for a daughter he loves.

But to Adrian it lands almost exactly on his own name. **Ria.** **Rian.** Three letters and one. The first time he hears it, he keeps walking until he reaches the stairs. Every morning, the man who cares for her most is calling out the boy who died, and he has no idea.

It never needs to be said out loud. Hold it back, and let the reader feel it long before she ever says it herself.

#### MOTIFS TO WEAVE IN

- **Salt and fuel.** The only things on the Moon that smell like the planet she was pulled off. At 2 AM she slips down to the sub-level transit bays and sits among the cargo loaders, breathing cold concrete and spilled fuel. She doesn't know what the machines are doing. She only knows the smell.
- **The pendant.** Worn every night, never spoken about, never explained to anyone.
- **"Ria."** Hold it back, and let it land with the reader long before she says it herself.
- **The transit bays.** The one place in the Lindemann house where she feels like herself, while everyone else thinks she's a frail heiress who tires easily.
- **The tide.** Where she came from, you didn't outrun the tide. You tied off and held on. Use it sparingly, as her private way of facing something.
- **The unanswered question.** The story never says what she inherited, what it does, or where it came from. Every time it shows up it's an effect with no mechanism: a reflex she never learned, a body that reacts before she thinks, an instinct that gets there first. The rules are yours to set. In canon.js this is the deliberately empty `talents` register.
